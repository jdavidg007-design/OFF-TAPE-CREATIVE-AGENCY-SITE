01 / Plan your video

Every great hype video starts with a plan.

Before picking up your camera, figure out what you're creating.

Start with a simple idea and build a storyline around it. Your story doesn't have to be complicated. You just need a beginning, middle, and end.

Think about what you're showing, how you're building excitement, and how you want your video to finish. For example, let's say you're creating a hype video of a sports car.

beginning

The driver grabs the keys, walks toward the car, and starts the engine.

middle

The car pulls away. You start showing driving sequences, close-up details, and different camera angles. The energy begins to build.

ending

The car arrives at its destination. The driver steps out, and you finish with a final hero shot.

That's your basic storyline. Now you can start building creative shots and transitions around it.

02 / Plan your fire moments

Don't wait until the edit to figure out your best shots.

Before your shoot, find 2–3 creative effects, transitions, or camera movements you want to include in your video.

This is what I like to call planning your fire moments. These are the shots or transitions that make your video stand out. You can find inspiration from other videos and study how certain effects or camera movements were created.

But here's the important part: you need to plan these moments BEFORE filming.

Some effects and transitions require specific camera movements, angles, framing, or multiple shots. If you don't plan them ahead of time, you might get to the editing process and realize you never captured the footage you needed.

That's why I like to choose my creative moments first and then build them into my shot list.

Example 1 · Match cut

A match cut connects two shots that share similar framing, composition, or movement.

  1. Decide which two shots you want to connect.
  2. Find a similar visual element or movement between them.
  3. Plan the framing for both shots.
  4. Make sure you capture the footage needed for the transition.

ExampleA close-up of a spinning car wheel transitions into another circular object.

Example 2 · Whip transition

A whip transition connects two shots using fast camera movement.

  1. Decide which two scenes you're connecting.
  2. Film the end of the first shot with a fast camera movement.
  3. Begin the second shot with a similar movement in the same direction.
  4. Use the motion blur to help hide the cut.

ExampleThe camera whips past a car, and the next shot reveals the vehicle in a completely different location.

Example 3 · Creative camera movement

Use a unique camera movement to make an important moment stand out.

  1. Choose the camera movement.
  2. Decide where the camera starts.
  3. Decide where the camera ends.
  4. Plan the equipment, space, or additional footage needed.

ExampleA dramatic FPV-style movement flies toward a car, circles around it, and reveals the entire vehicle.

03 / Build your shot list

Shoot with a purpose, not just a camera.

Now that you have your storyline and fire moments planned, it's time to figure out what you need to film.

The secret to having more creative freedom during editing is capturing a variety of shots. Don't film everything from the same angle. Get wide shots, close-ups, different perspectives, and creative camera movements. This gives you more options when you're putting your video together.

Here are some shots I recommend capturing.

1

Wide shots

Wide shots establish your location and show the full scene.

ExampleA wide shot of a sports car parked in an empty lot.

2

Close-ups

Close-ups capture the little details that make a big difference.

ExampleA hand grabbing the keys, a wheel spinning, or headlights turning on.

3

Low-angle shots

Place the camera low and point it upward. This can make your subject look bigger, stronger, and more powerful.

ExampleA low-angle shot of the front of a sports car.

4

High-angle shots

Place your camera above the subject and angle downward. This gives viewers a different perspective.

ExampleAn overhead view of the vehicle and surrounding location.

5

Camera movements

Use different camera movements to bring energy into your footage.

ExamplesPush-in · Pull-out · Orbit · Tracking movement · Creative FPV-style movement

04 / Shoot your video

Turn your plan into footage.

Now it's time to bring your ideas to life.

Use the shot list you created as a guide. Start by filming the shots you need to tell your story. Capture your beginning, middle, and ending.

Then focus on your creative moments. Remember those effects and transitions you planned earlier? This is where they come to life. Make sure you're capturing the exact angles, framing, and movements needed to make them work.

And don't be afraid to experiment. Sometimes your best shots happen when you try something unexpected.

    05 / Build your edit

    This is where everything comes together.

    Now that you've captured your footage, it's time to bring your hype video to life.

    One of the first things I look for is music. You can choose your song before filming or during editing. I personally like finding music while I'm editing. I look for songs that build anticipation and have a strong beat drop.

    Why? Because music helps me decide how to build energy throughout the video. I usually break my edit into four parts.

    part 1

    The hook

    Grab attention. Start with something that immediately makes people want to keep watching. It could be:

    • A crazy camera angle.
    • An unexpected moment.
    • A powerful close-up.
    • A creative transition.
    • A unique visual reveal.

    The goal is to make the viewer interested in what happens next.

    part 2

    The build-up

    Create anticipation. Start developing the story. Introduce your subject and show what's happening.

    As the music builds, increase the energy. Use your shots to lead viewers toward your biggest moment.

    part 3

    The beat drop

    Bring the hype. When the beat drops, this is where the energy takes off. Bring in your:

    • Fire moments.
    • Creative transitions.
    • Dynamic camera movements.
    • Strongest shots.
    • Faster cuts.

    Use the music to help emphasize important visual moments.

    part 4

    The ending

    Finish strong. Bring your video to a satisfying conclusion. End with a powerful shot, creative reveal, or final moment that completes your story.

    Don't let the energy disappear at the end.

    Your edit timeline

    hook → build-up → beat drop → ending

    These timings are only examples. Different videos and songs may need different pacing.

    OFF-TAPE tip

    Don't just cut randomly to the beat. Build anticipation, create energy, and make your biggest moments hit at the right time.

    06 / Make it hit with sound design

    The little sounds can make a big difference.

    Sound design is one of my favorite parts of editing.

    The right sound effects can make your shots feel more powerful, your camera movements smoother, and your biggest moments more impactful. Here are three sound effects I commonly use.

    1

    Risers

    Risers help create tension and anticipation. They're great for building toward an important moment.

    ExampleA rising sound effect leading into the beat drop of your song.

    2

    Impacts

    Impact sounds emphasize powerful moments.

    ExampleA heavy impact sound as a car is revealed or a major transition happens.

    3

    Whooshes

    Whooshes help accent camera movements and transitions.

    ExampleA whoosh as your camera whips from one scene to another.

    bonus

    Natural sounds

    Sometimes the best sound effects come from the environment. Engines starting, footsteps, doors closing, tires rolling, clothing movement, mechanical sounds. These details can make your footage feel more alive.
